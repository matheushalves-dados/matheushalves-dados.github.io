Case 02 · Digital product

Where a personal finance portal loses its sign-ups

A funnel from first visit to completed partner sign-up, split by channel and device, to show exactly which step leaves money on the table.

My role
Instrumentation, modeling and analysis
Tools
GA4, BigQuery, dbt, SQL, Looker Studio
Audience
Product, marketing and content
Context
Work I did at iDinheiro

The problem

Each team looked at its own slice of the funnel, and nobody saw the whole chain. When sign-ups dropped, the explanations came without numbers.

What I did

An event plan in GA4, raw events in BigQuery and a session-level table in dbt, joined with sign-ups confirmed by partners.

What it answers

Which step to fix first, on which channel and device, and how many sign-ups each page leaves behind.

The numbers are synthetic, generated for demonstration, and the page titles are examples. The approach is the same one I used in the GA4 migration and the BigQuery event integration. The dashboard was rebuilt in HTML; at work, I used Power BI and Looker Studio.

The problem

A portal that earns money by recommending financial products depends on a long chain: someone arrives from a search, reads the content, clicks the comparison tool, goes to the partner’s site and completes the sign-up there. Each team watched its own part: SEO watched sessions, content watched reading, sales watched sign-ups.

Nobody saw the whole chain. So when sign-ups fell, every team had a different explanation, and none came with a number.

How I approached it

Instrument before analyzing

During the GA4 migration, I defined an event plan for each funnel step, with standardized names and parameters. Without it, any analysis would be a guess.

Bring raw data into the warehouse

Events land in BigQuery and are modeled in dbt with full history. That makes it possible to join on-site behavior with partner-confirmed sign-ups, something the GA4 interface can’t do.

Slice until you find the cause

The biggest percentage drop is between reading and clicking, which is expected for a content portal. The drop most worth fixing is another one: going from the comparison tool to sign-up on mobile, where the rate is far below desktop. The funnel average hides it; the device split reveals it.

    The model behind it

    GA4 exports raw events to BigQuery, one event per row with nested parameters. In dbt, I turn that into one row per session with the furthest step reached, the channel and the device. From there, any funnel is a single group by.

    This kind of transformation is what cut daily report processing from about 2 hours to 30 minutes at iDinheiro: the heavy lifting happens once in the warehouse, not on every dashboard refresh.

    models/marts/fct_sessao_funil.sql

    with eventos as (
    select
        user_pseudo_id,
        (select value.int_value from unnest(event_params)
          where key = 'ga_session_id')   as sessao_id,
        event_name,
        traffic_source.medium           as canal,
        device.category                 as dispositivo
    from {{ ref('stg_ga4_eventos') }}
)
select
    sessao_id, canal, dispositivo,
    max(case event_name
        when 'cadastro_concluido'  then 4
        when 'cadastro_iniciado'   then 3
        when 'clique_comparador'   then 2
        when 'scroll_50'           then 1
        else 0 end)                 as etapa_maxima
from eventos
group by 1, 2, 3
    Metric dictionary
    Session
    A visit identified by GA4’s ga_session_id. One person can have several sessions.
    Read the content
    Sessions that reached 50% of the page (scroll_50 event).
    Clicked the comparison tool
    Sessions with at least one click on an offer or on the comparison tool.
    Started sign-up
    Sessions that reached the partner’s form, confirmed by the partner’s callback.
    Conversion rate
    Completed sign-ups divided by total sessions in the slice.
    Sign-ups to recover
    Page sessions multiplied by the gap between the portal’s average conversion and the page’s conversion.
    What I would do next
    • Run an A/B test with a shorter mobile form, measuring completed sign-ups, not just clicks.
    • Set up an automatic alert for when the comparison-to-sign-up step drops more than 10% in a week.
    • Bring each partner’s revenue per sign-up into the model, to prioritize pages by revenue rather than volume.
