Case 03 · Data engineering

Modernizing a data platform without stopping operations

Technical leadership of the migration from a legacy SSIS ETL to an architecture on GCP, dbt and Databricks: versioned, tested and cheaper to run.

My role
Technical lead for the migration
Tools
GCP, Databricks, dbt, SQL, Python
Business goal
About R$ 500K in savings next year
Status
In progress, at Ancar

The problem

Business logic hidden in SSIS packages, with no version control or tests, running on a server that costs money even when idle.

What I’m doing

Rewriting the logic as versioned, tested dbt models, with on-demand processing on Databricks over GCP.

How we measure it

Monthly cost, data availability time, data incidents and time to ship a new KPI.

The architecture here is simplified and contains no internal figures. This case describes the approach and the decisions, not company data.

Why migrate

ETLs built in SSIS over many years tend to share the same symptoms: business rules hidden inside packages that only one or two people understand, no version control, no automated tests and a server that costs money even when it sits idle.

For the business, this shows up as delays and distrust. Every new KPI takes weeks, and when two reports show different numbers, nobody can say which one is right.

What changes

The logic moves out of the packages and into SQL models in dbt, versioned in Git and reviewed like code. Every model has tests and auto-generated documentation, and you can trace where each column comes from.

Processing moves to on-demand compute on Databricks, on top of GCP. Instead of paying for a server that is always on, the company pays for what it processes. That is where much of the savings target comes from.

Decisions

The choices that matter most.

The biggest risk in a migration is breaking a number someone uses to make decisions.

Migrate by domain, not all at once

Each business area migrates as a complete block, from source to dashboard. Every delivery creates value on its own and risk stays contained.

Rewrite instead of translating

Copying package by package would carry old problems into the new platform. The migration is the chance to review every rule and drop what nobody uses.

Run in parallel and reconcile

The legacy system is only switched off once the new platform’s numbers match the old ones, or once every difference is explained and approved by the team that owns the data.

Cost as an architecture requirement

Partitioning, incremental processing and auto-terminating clusters are part of the design from day one, not a fix after the bill arrives.

Plan

Four stages, from inventory to shutdown.

  1. 01 · inventory

    Map

    Packages, dependencies and who uses each output. Prioritize by business impact and technical risk.

  2. 02 · foundation

    Standardize

    Environments, CI, naming conventions and dbt layers. The pattern every new model will follow.

  3. 03 · migration

    Migrate by domain

    Rewrite, test, run in parallel and reconcile each domain with the team that owns the data.

  4. 04 · shutdown

    Shut down and measure

    Switch off the legacy system, optimize costs and measure results against the savings target.

Quality that doesn’t depend on someone remembering

Every model declares its own quality rules. If a load brings a duplicate key or an out-of-range value, the pipeline stops and alerts before the data reaches a dashboard.

It is the same principle as the shopping center dashboard: the KPI is calculated and tested once, in the data layer, and every consumer gets the same number.

models/marts/schema.yml

models:
  - name: fct_lojista_mensal
    description: Vendas, ABL e custo de ocupação por loja e mês.
    columns:
      - name: loja_mes_id
        tests: [unique, not_null]
      - name: abl_m2
        tests:
          - not_null
          - dbt_utils.accepted_range:
              min_value: 1
      - name: vendas
        tests:
          - dbt_utils.accepted_range:
              min_value: 0

How we measure success

Four KPIs, defined before we started.

A migration is only done when the numbers show the gain.

↓ lower is better

Monthly platform cost

What the company pays each month to process and store data. This is where the savings target shows up.

↓ earlier is better

Data availability time

What time the previous day’s data is ready for the business to use.

↓ lower is better

Data incidents

How many times a wrong or late number reached a report.

↓ lower is better

Time to a new KPI

Days between a KPI request and it being available, tested and documented.
