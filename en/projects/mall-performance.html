Case 01 · Retail

Performance dashboard for a shopping center network

One screen that answers the three questions leadership asks every Monday: are sales growing, where are they growing, and which tenants need attention before it becomes a problem.

My role
Analysis, modeling and visualization
Tools
SQL, dbt, BigQuery, Power BI
Audience
Leadership, leasing and operations
Context
The industry I work in today, at Ancar

The problem

Sales, leases and foot traffic live in separate systems, and a tenant’s decline only shows up in the quarterly report.

What I did

A dimensional model in dbt with the industry’s KPIs and automated tests, plus a dashboard that crosses sales decline with occupancy cost.

What it answers

Whether sales are growing, in which categories, and which stores need a conversation with the leasing team now.

All numbers in this dashboard are synthetic, generated for demonstration, and the malls are fictional. The structure, KPIs and business questions reflect the kind of work I do in this industry; no company data was used. The dashboard was rebuilt in HTML to run in the browser. At work, I build this kind of dashboard in Power BI.

The problem

At a shopping center operator, the information exists but lives in silos: tenant-reported sales in one system, leases and billing in another, foot traffic in a third. Each team builds its own spreadsheet, and Monday’s meeting starts with an argument about which number is right.

The cost isn’t just time. When a tenant’s decline only appears in the end-of-quarter report, the conversation is already about delinquency or a store closing, not about how to help them sell more.

How I approached it

Start from the decision, not the data

Before opening any table, I listed the decisions the dashboard must support: tenant mix reviews, lease renegotiations and campaign planning. Anything that didn’t help those decisions was left out.

KPIs the industry already uses

Sales per m², occupancy cost and delinquency are the language of mall operators. Using these KPIs, each with a single documented definition, ends the debate about which number is right.

From alert to action

The at-risk stores table crosses sales decline with high occupancy cost. A single signal may be seasonal; both together point to a tenant at real risk of leaving.

    The model behind it

    The dashboard reads from a simple dimensional model built in dbt on BigQuery: a monthly fact table per store, plus store, mall and calendar dimensions.

    Two choices make the difference. First, KPIs are calculated in the data layer, not inside the dashboard, so Power BI, a spreadsheet or an API all show exactly the same number. Second, automated dbt tests check for unique keys, non-null leasable area and non-negative sales, so a bad load is stopped before it reaches leadership.

    models/marts/fct_lojista_mensal.sql

    with vendas as (
    select loja_id,
           date_trunc(data_venda, month) as mes,
           sum(valor_venda) as vendas
    from {{ ref('stg_vendas_lojistas') }}
    group by 1, 2
)
select
    v.loja_id, v.mes, l.shopping_id, l.segmento,
    v.vendas,
    safe_divide(v.vendas, l.abl_m2)        as venda_m2,
    safe_divide(c.custo_ocupacao, v.vendas) as custo_ocupacao_pct,
    safe_divide(v.vendas, lag(v.vendas, 12)
        over (partition by v.loja_id order by v.mes)) - 1
                                            as var_ano_anterior
from vendas v
join {{ ref('dim_lojas') }} l using (loja_id)
left join {{ ref('int_custo_ocupacao') }} c
    using (loja_id, mes)
    Metric dictionary
    Sales per m²
    Tenant-reported monthly sales divided by occupied GLA (gross leasable area). Lets you compare stores and malls of different sizes.
    Occupancy cost
    Rent, condo fees and marketing fund divided by the store’s sales. Above 15–16%, a tenant’s operation tends to be under pressure.
    Occupancy rate
    Leased GLA divided by the mall’s total GLA on the last day of the month.
    Delinquency
    Amounts more than 30 days overdue divided by the amount billed in the month.
    Sales per visitor
    Total sales divided by foot traffic. Separates growth from more visitors from growth from higher spend per visit.
    What I would do next
    • Automatically send the list of at-risk stores to each mall’s leasing manager every Monday.
    • Build a simple tenant churn-risk model using sales history, occupancy cost and late payments.
    • Measure the effect of renegotiations: did stores with revised leases start growing again?
